\section{2. The canonical typed process and the regime boundary}
Let the strategic state, world state, and joint action spaces be measurable spaces S, X, and A. The current state is y=(s,x). An action law alpha(y,da) is followed by a physical transition P(y,a,dx'), then a strategic update U(y,a,x',ds'). Their ordered composition determines the joint kernel K on S times X. This order matters: an update can observe the realized next world when the model permits it, but the X1 reinforcement example deliberately reads the old world. Merely presenting a transition matrix does not establish correspondence with the declared strategic primitives.

Feasible-graph specifications must be extended measurably before they define kernels on full product spaces. The same discipline applies to U and P: a fixed law on a nonempty target space supplies the declared off-graph extension where needed. Such a completion is part of the model, not evidence that off-graph behavior is substantively identified. Error bounds and intervention comparisons must include rows reachable under the compared interventions, even when those rows have zero probability under baseline data.

Freeze Sigma=(B,B0,h,nu,c): a measurable regime region B, a measurable initial basin B0 contained in B, a measurable descriptor h on the whole state space, a target probability nu on the descriptor space, and an explicitly declared convergence mode c. The reference measure used for region nontriviality is frozen separately. The immutable specification is supplied before classification; the formal API does not infer historical preregistration from already constructed values.

Exact GR has four clauses: the canonical process is well posed for every initial probability law; Assumption4.1 holds; every state y in B has K(y,B)=1; and the descriptor empirical occupations converge to nu in mode c for every initial probability supported on B0. Assumption4.1 requires a nonempty basin, nonempty region interior or positive region reference mass, at least two descriptor values somewhere on B, and two basin point-start baseline path laws that differ. Basin-reachable descriptor nondegeneracy is an additional confirmatory-GR requirement, not a fifth universal Exact-GR clause.

Constitutive comparisons freeze a measurable B1 contained in B0 with TWO starts inducing distinct baseline point-start path laws. Randomness within one starting law does not satisfy that condition. An admissible strategic replacement holds P fixed. For a frozen property psi, constitution means baseline and intervened values differ at every y in B1. Uniform constitution is stronger: the infimum over B1 of their metric distance is positive, supplying a common positive lower bound. Pointwise positive effects can still have infimum zero. Neither property effects nor positive uniform margins supply Exact-GR persistence or convergence.

Confirmatory use also needs substantive descriptor grounding. When a property reads the descriptor after leaving the regime region, the off-region descriptor must be frozen and justified by the relevance map. A decorative extension outside the region cannot earn a grounded constitutive interpretation merely because it changes a measurable output. These requirements remain separate from the existence and uniqueness of the canonical path law and from the formal convenience of a finite state space.

\section{3. Exact first-exit routing and canonical restart}
Exit routing records where a process first leaves a declared region, without conditioning away trajectories that never leave. For a start inside B, the finite routing submeasure sums the mutually exclusive events whose first exit occurs at transitions one through L. Its mass at a destination set depends only on the destination outside B. Adding the surviving mass after L transitions gives one. The countable limit retains a separate never-exit term. This accounting prevents normalization from falsely turning a rare exit into an almost-sure destination.

C69 proves that the existing canonical future after a finite history agrees with restart from the history's last state. The proof first matches every shifted finite-prefix law using stationarity of the canonical transition construction. It then applies projective uniqueness of the already constructed infinite path law. Thus the result discharges the earlier restart interface instead of postulating an independent strong-Markov axiom or rebuilding a second trajectory law.

The stopping argument decomposes by a concrete finite first-exit time. Each finite piece restarts from the unnormalized arrival submeasure; summing those pieces gives the infinite routing formula. No expression evaluates the stopped state at an infinite exit time. Histories that remain forever inside B retain exactly their own mass. The generic result works for the measurable spaces of the encoded canonical model and does not insert a finite-state or standard-Borel assumption into this particular restart theorem.

Quasi-stationary destination formulas require their own hypotheses. When a QSD has one-step retention theta strictly between zero and one, the destination distribution is the one-step exit mass divided by one minus theta. At theta equal to one there is no exiting mass to normalize. None of these formulas says that arrival in another set implies remaining there: target invariance is an independent premise of an eventual-switch claim.

\companionEquation{R_B^{(L)}(y,Y\setminus B)+\mathbb{P}_y(\tau_B>L)=1,\qquad R_B^{(\infty)}(y,Y\setminus B)+\mathbb{P}_y(\tau_B=\infty)=1.}
{\small Finite and infinite exit accounting preserve the nonexit alternative.}

\section{4. Random entry, occupation limits, and successful switching}
An occupation statement certified from deterministic target starts must be transported carefully to entry at a random time and state. C70 first expresses the canonical path law as a bind over its initial law. A positive-mass arrival submeasure is normalized into a genuine probability supported on the target basin. The target certificate applies to that law, and multiplication by the arrival mass recovers the stopped-future statement. Zero-mass arrivals are handled separately by the zero measure.

The whole-path conclusion then requires deletion of the finite prefix preceding entry. For a bounded observable with absolute value at most C, deleting k observations changes the appropriately aligned averages by at most 2kC divided by k+T. This explicit estimate vanishes for every realized finite k. The argument preserves a weak occupation limit without presuming a measurable set of convergent paths on an arbitrary descriptor space. Almost-everywhere transport is used directly, and the final event has the corresponding outer-measure interpretation where needed.

The supported in-probability statement is defined through bounded continuous test integrals. On the routed event, the probability of a fixed positive deviation tends to zero. If routing and retention have failure budget eta, the unconditional eventual deviation bound contains eta; it need not vanish when eta is positive. The research mode is not presented as an unproved equivalence with a metric definition of convergence in probability on all probability measures.

In almostSureWeak mode, a successful-switch event combines routing, target retention, and occupation with probability at least one minus eta. The in-probability mode instead has the deviation bounds just stated, alongside the retention bound. The original source-survival property has constitutive margin at least 1-eta.toReal in both modes. Grounded uniform PR adds eta below one and all original regime, grounding, and confirmatory gates. A custom whole-path convergence predicate additionally needs finite-prefix invariance.

\section{5. Finite-horizon perturbations and approximate quotients}
A2 propagates primitive errors through the actual ordered composition. With declared uniform event-total-variation budgets for alpha, P, and U, the induced one-step error is at most their sum, capped at one. The comparison may allow different physical kernels between two models; within each model its strategic intervention must still satisfy that model's fixed-world admissibility condition. A changed world is not relabeled as a strategic replacement.

Finite-prefix propagation starts from a common initial state and counts T transitions. It yields quantitative property and margin bounds only when the frozen property is a bounded finite-prefix score or carries the explicit Lipschitz certificate used by the theorem. Baseline and intervention errors contribute separate budgets. The resulting finite-persistence transport still needs a nonvacuous survival floor and a margin greater than the relevant errors. Clamping a persistence-error estimate to one does not prove a useful persistence guarantee.

E1 replaces exact type-respecting intertwining by a uniform one-step projection defect. The fine projected process may retain hidden memory and need not be Markov. A last-step hybrid compares its next-step extension with the coarse kernel under the same projected history law, then compares history laws under the same coarse kernel. Pushforward contraction and the TV triangle inequality give the additive induction, without assuming a disintegration over quotient fibres.

The margin theorem further requires the fine property to factor through the compressed prefix law, a declared TV-Lipschitz coarse property, and compatible original comparison sets. Exact zero defect recovers equal margins in that scope. Region, basin, descriptor, relevance, and convergence compatibility remain independent conditions for Exact-GR or PR preservation. A tiny leakage defect can destroy exact invariance while leaving a positive finite-horizon margin. The sharper geometric quotient envelope remains open as a general Lean theorem.

\section{6. Sequential attribution and multi-agent protocol scope}
Sequential intervention analysis compares four genuine corners: baseline, action replacement, update replacement, and their jointly admissible replacement. All use the same frozen world primitive and property map. The metric triangle inequality bounds the joint effect by the baseline action effect plus the conditional update effect after the action replacement. The symmetric order is a separate comparison. The resulting conditional margins are not generally obtained by subtracting singleton baseline margins.

Joint-only constitution illustrates why pointwise statements matter. Both singleton effects can vanish at every comparison state while the joint margin is positive. An infimum equal to zero is weaker: a singleton intervention can have positive effect at some starts and still have zero margin. The accepted B2 witness reuses the existing Boolean persistence construction. Vector interaction identities require a normed additive output space; a scalar metric remainder is not automatically an interaction vector or a causal mediation effect.

F1 makes closed-loop and declared open-loop response protocols explicit. In the strict opponent-locked semantics, an opponent anchor's action and update may use its own state and the exogenous common component permitted by the specification. Endogenous public feedback is excluded when it carries the treated player's response channel. Baseline agreement at one start does not authorize treating a reactive opponent as frozen after an intervention reaches a different state.

The unresolved strict MA2 obligation must remain visible. The earlier state-feedback example establishes a broader response phenomenon but does not witness strict opponent-locked MA2. The repaired Boolean deterministic recording toy proves a scoped obstruction: strict anchors agreeing with baseline at the designated start give identical full open-loop laws for any treated action rule. That is neither a general impossibility theorem for stochastic anchors nor a completion of the required witness. Exogenous-common-process agreement conditions are sufficient conditions within their stated class, not a necessary characterization.
