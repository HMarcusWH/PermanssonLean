# Exact finite one-step observation-conditioned control (numeric)

`one-step-belief` is an explicitly bounded precursory profile for the partial-observation research program. It is **not** the general finite-horizon hidden-state belief-tree or alpha-vector optimality theorem.

The caller supplies a complete finite `α/P/U` strategic-world model with exactly one strategic state and a hidden physical world `X`, a frozen initial law `µ(x)`, a finite observation alphabet, a complete observation channel `Q(o|x)` and a terminal reward `r(x')` after one physical transition. The timeline is **initial hidden world → observation → admissible action shared by all currently possible hidden states → physical/world update**. The pre-existing `α` policy is part of the validated source model, but the calculation explicitly optimizes a new observation-contingent action law; it does not assert an equilibrium or an identified external policy intervention.

For each reachable observation `o`, the engine computes the exact mass `p(o)=Σ_x µ(x)Q(o|x)`, Bayes posterior `p(x|o)=µ(x)Q(o|x)/p(o)`, and the action-specific **observation-mass-weighted** expected terminal reward under the original `P/U` rows (no division by the observation probability is needed for choosing an argmax). An action is legal only if it is admissible in **every hidden state with positive posterior support** for that observation. The engine picks an argmax and records all tied argmax actions. A zero-probability observation is marked **unreachable**, with no posterior and no selected action; it is never silently normalized or treated as observed evidence.

The independent checker does not import the numerical belief producer. It reconstructs the input model, likelihoods and posteriors, uses the source `P/U` laws for every selected observed action, **enumerates the entire bounded deterministic observation-policy family**, and checks that the returned policy is optimal within that class. It permits a different tied optimum when the returned next-state law matches its actual action choices. For a meaningful blind-policy benchmark, it computes the best single action legal in all positive-prior hidden states; if none exists, both blind-policy reward and value-of-observation comparison are explicitly `null`.

The sample contains two hidden states (healthy/damaged), observations (green/red/unseen) and actions (leave/repair). The observation-conditioned policy chooses **leave after green** and **repair after red**. It achieves exact expected reward **13/16**, versus **3/4** for the best unobserved control, giving an exact one-step value of information **1/16**. The `unseen` signal is unreachable and intentionally has no posterior. A separating test restricts legal actions to distinct hidden supports and confirms that perfectly revealing observations are permitted while a mixed observation without a common feasible action is rejected.

**Scope and scientific limits:** one strategic state; up to three physical states, three actions and three observations; one observed choice and one transition; fully declared rational inputs; no inference about reality, endogenous observation model, repeated Bayesian filtering, hidden memory, infinite-horizon value, causal identification, Exact GR/PR status, independent mathematical CERT or publication approval.
