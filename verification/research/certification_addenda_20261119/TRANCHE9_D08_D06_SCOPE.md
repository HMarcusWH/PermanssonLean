## Original D-08: admissibility and compatibility

Registry purpose: **Illegal/conflicting grammar block selected by controller.**
Expected result: **No admissible typed controlled row.**

The historical restricted Boolean menu already admits an action replacement
and an update replacement separately, and excludes their joint block under its
frozen component-name predicate. Its singleton lists do not themselves exhibit
two distinct overlapping action patches.

The new probe constructs a concrete compatible-overlap `CertifiedMenu` on the
same original Boolean strategic/world source. A localized false-action patch
and the existing full-domain false-action patch have different regions, a
nonempty overlap, and equal action rows wherever they overlap. They coexist in
one certified choice. The physical kernel, state indexing, grammar admission
predicate, rational rows and separate update choice are retained. Named facts
identify the actual alpha-P-U induced row, its exact rational correspondence,
the fixed world kernel, and normalized typed controlled rows.

A localized true-action patch conflicts with the localized false-action patch.
No choice in any `CertifiedMenu` on this source can contain both, because the
menu's compatibility field would contradict their actual action rows.

This is a statement about **certified choices**. Raw folds of normalized Markov
patches still exist; the new proof explicitly shows that the two conflicting
orders differ. Normalization alone does not establish certified admission.
The localized overlapping patch is a `RowPatch`, not an added atom in the
disjoint-domain `AtomicBank`; no atomic-bank extension or universal grammar
theorem is claimed.

The separate fine-grammar OR fixture has joint margin 1 and singleton margins
0. This is a joint-only **positive-margin** witness, not a grammar that admits
only the joint. The restricted grammar separately excludes that joint. The
two grammar scopes must not be exchanged.

Three false controls try to admit the forbidden joint, claim the conflicting
pair is compatible, and give the fine-grammar action singleton margin 1.
They must fail for mathematical proof/type reasons.

## Original D-06: reachable histories and augmented beliefs

Registry purpose: **Same physical belief but different hidden completed flags.**
Expected result: **Full augmented belief required.**

The source already contains a finite chronological law with one fixed initial
prior and a fair observation-admissible choice between two actions. Both
action/observation histories have probability 1/2 in that same law. The
physical readout is the same Unit value, while the posterior probability of
the hidden completed flag is respectively 0 and 1. The controller observes
its own chosen action as part of the history.

The new probe connects these exact reachable posteriors to immediate terminal
completion values 0 and 1, and proves that no value function of the current
Unit readout alone can represent both. It also gives stable names to the
original observation-information, illegal hidden-peek, zero-likelihood and
successor-readout-order fixtures.

This counterexample establishes current-readout insufficiency. It does not
prove that an explicit full posterior is the unique or minimal sufficient
representation; an equivalent statistic may retain the relevant information.
General augmented-belief sufficiency is audited separately through the actual
filter/history correspondence, finite observation-policy domination and
attainment, and the original monitored-control adapter.

The scope is finite hidden/control/observation spaces, finite horizons, real
behavioral histories of the controller's own controls and readouts, and the
adapter's common legal menu. A hidden-state controller, arbitrary measurable
spaces and infinite-horizon sufficiency are not supplied by this packet.
The older fixture with two different priors remains a distinct statement.

Three false controls equate the two reachable completion values, replace the
observation-admissible optimum 1/2 with the illegal hidden-peek value 1, and
give a zero-likelihood branch a nonzero expected contribution.
